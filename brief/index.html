Study protocol for preoperative classification using integrated screening and short-course neoadjuvant BRAF/MEK inhibition in newly diagnosed papillary craniopharyngioma (the PRECISE-PCP study): a prospective single-arm study

7.3

The PRECISE-PCP study is a prospective, single-arm protocol designed to classify newly diagnosed papillary craniopharyngioma (PCP) by integrating comprehensive screening with short-course neoadjuvant BRAF/MEK inhibition. Given that most PCP harbor the BRAF mutation, this approach aims to improve preoperative classification accuracy, which is crucial because the two main subtypes, ACP and PCP, require distinct management strategies. The study will evaluate the efficacy and safety of this integrated treatment approach for better patient stratification.

Why it matters: This protocol advances personalized neuro-oncology by optimizing preoperative classification and treatment for challenging craniopharyngioma subtypes.

Capillary-based Subcellular Sampling Uncovers the Stress Granule Proteome in Single Cells

5.0

We introduce a novel capillary-based subcellular sampling technique enabling the isolation of specific cellular compartments, such as stress granules, from single cells. This method allows for the direct analysis of the proteome within these biomolecular condensates. Our application successfully uncovered the detailed proteomic composition of stress granules in single cells, providing unprecedented insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying cellular stress responses.

Why it matters: This technique advances single-cell proteomics by enabling high-resolution analysis of specific, functionally critical subcellular compartments.
